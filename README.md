# swift-acoustics-radiation

Use this package when you need the sound a vibrating body sends into the air: a lute's bowl, a guitar's body, a box, or a board in a wall. Give `Acoustics.Radiation.Body` the body's surface as a swift-mesh `Mesh<Point<3, Double>>` of triangles in metres, closed for a body in free air (`.free`) or standing on a rigid plane (`.baffled`). Then `Acoustics.Radiation.Field(body, frequency:, velocity:, air:)` takes one motion of that surface at one frequency, such as a mode's, as a normal velocity at every vertex, and gives the pressure on every face, the power radiated, the radiation efficiency, and the pressure at any point (`pressure(at:)`) or far away in any direction (`pressure(toward:at:)`). It solves the Kirchhoff–Helmholtz boundary integral equation by collocation on flat triangles, with swift-galerkin's triangle rules (the one around a face's own centroid among them), and pins its solution at every frequency with points inside the body (CHIEF). It needs no meshing of the air, so a closed body radiates as easily as a flat one; swift-acoustics-plate's baffled Rayleigh integral is the special case of a flat board in a wall.

## Laws

- **Amplitudes.** Every amplitude is complex, for time e^{−iωt}: a value A stands for Re(A e^{−iωt}). Pressures are `Pressure.Value<Complex.Number<Double>>` in pascals; velocities are real, in metres per second along the surface's normal into the air, all in phase, as a mode's are.
- **Surface.** Faces are triangles with area; anything else fails with `.face(index)`. They run counterclockwise seen from the air; a surface listed inside out, by the sign of the volume it encloses, is turned. In `.baffled` the air fills z > 0 above a rigid plane z = 0; a vertex below it fails with `.vertex(index)`, and a flat board in the plane faces up. A velocity per vertex is required (`.velocity`) and the frequency must be positive and finite (`.frequency`).
- **Discretization.** The pressure is constant over each face and the velocity linear; each face's equation is taken at its centroid. The 1/R part of a face's own integral is integrated in closed form and the rest by a rule collapsed at the centroid; a face near the point that sees it is cut into pieces until each is at least twice its size away. Faces should be small next to the wavelength: six or more to a wavelength.
- **Uniqueness.** The Kirchhoff–Helmholtz equation alone has no unique solution where the body's inside, held at zero pressure, would resonate. The same integral must vanish inside the body, and it is asked to at points inward from evenly spaced faces, part of the way to the far side (Schenck 1968); all equations are solved together in least squares, through the normal equations of a dense complex matrix. A flat board has no inside and needs none.
- **Baffle.** Every face has its image in the plane added, which makes the plane rigid. A face lying in the plane is its own image, sees the whole half-space, and its equation is Rayleigh's integral.
- **Power and efficiency.** `power` is ½ Re ∫ p v dS; `efficiency` is it over ρ₀ c times ∫ v² dS / 2, the definition swift-acoustics-plate uses. Pressure is linear in the velocity, so the efficiency does not depend on its scale. A body s times as large at 1/s the frequency radiates alike.
- **Far field.** `pressure(toward:at:)` is e^{ikr}/4πr ∫ (−ik (r̂ · n) p − iωρ₀ v) e^{−ik r̂ · y} dS, measured from the origin; it agrees with `pressure(at:)` far away, and its power through a far sphere is `power`.
- **Checked against** closed forms and the plate. A sphere's flat faces lie inside it, so a faceted sphere is compared with the sphere of equal volume:
  - **Pulsating sphere,** 224 faces: efficiency (ka)²/(1 + (ka)²) within 2%, the surface pressure ρ₀c ika/(ika − 1) within 2%, and the pressure at 2a and at 100a in three directions within 3%, for ka = 0.5, 2 and π, where the inside resonates.
  - **Oscillating sphere,** 224 faces: efficiency (ka)⁴/(4 + (ka)⁴) within 2.5% and the far field's cos θ within 0.02, at ka = 1 and 4.49, the first zero of j₁. From 120 faces to 224 the error falls as the faces' area does: second order.
  - **Hemisphere on a baffle,** pulsating: as the whole sphere, within 2% in efficiency and surface pressure and 3% far away, along the baffle too.
  - **Baffled piston:** 1 − J₁(2ka)/ka within 1% for ka from 0.5 to 2, for the disk of the mesh's area.
  - **swift-acoustics-plate:** a simply supported Sitka spruce panel's first four modes radiate as the plate's own Rayleigh integral gives, within 3%.
  - **Laws:** the power through a far sphere is the surface's within 2%; `pressure(at:)` far away is `pressure(toward:at:)` within 1%; linearity, scaling and turning a surface inside out hold to rounding.
